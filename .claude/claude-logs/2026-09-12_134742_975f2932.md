Short answer: it probably isn't. The on-probe per-base depth looks the same in both classes — what differs is how much of each locus is buffer.

**The mechanism**

Every locus is one probe's BLAST hit plus a *fixed* 300 bp buffer on each side ([01d_filter-loci.R:80-84](scripts/01d_filter-loci.R#L80-L84), `buffer_size=300` in [1_main.md:5](run/1_main.md#L5)). And `mean_dp` for a locus comes from 04a, which averages over every position of that interval, buffer included ([05a_prep-qc-tables.R:118](scripts/05a_prep-qc-tables.R#L118)).

The probe set has two length classes, so the buffer is a wildly different share of each:

| class | probe hit | interval | on-probe share | median `mean_dp` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| short | 200 bp | 800 bp | 25% | 29.8 |
| long | ~1,950 bp | ~2,550 bp | 77% | 63.8 |

Fitting `mean_dp × interval = D·probe_len + F·600` across retained loci gives **D ≈ 78× on probe, F ≈ 15× in the buffer**. Those two constants reproduce 29.8 and 63.8 on their own. Short loci aren't captured worse per base — they're three-quarters shoulder.

**Direct test of the "more probes" story**

Within the long class alone, probe length spans 1,000–2,015 bp and correlates with depth at **r = −0.07**. If more probe bought better per-base capture, depth would keep climbing there. It doesn't.
