Verdict: the idea is sound, the metric is the weak part, and there are a few implementation issues worth fixing. I ran the numbers on your current data to check.

## Is the step a good idea?

Yes in principle — excess heterozygosity is the standard signature of collapsed paralogs in target-capture data, and you should filter on it before any popgen/phylogenetic analysis.

But two things about *how* it's defined here:

**1. The percentile default removes 1% of loci by construction.** The observed distribution (I computed it from `results/consensus/stats/vcf/`, 1762 loci × 177 samples):

```
min=0.00005  p25=0.0024  med=0.0034  p75=0.0048  p90=0.0062  p95=0.0072  p99=0.0099  max=0.0329
```

It's continuous with no clean break. `--max_het_pctile 99` cuts at 0.0099 and drops 18 loci, most of them only ~3× the median — i.e. plausibly just normal high-diversity loci. Only the top ~4-5 loci (5-10× median) look like genuine outliers. An absolute cutoff informed by the distribution is more defensible than a quantile; the script supports `--max_het_density`, so I'd use that.

**2. Mean het density per locus is a diluted proxy.** The real paralog signature is per-*site*: a site where a large fraction of samples are heterozygous. Averaging het calls over locus length dilutes a handful of fixed-het sites in a long locus and inflates short ones. Since `04c_merge-vcf.sh` merges the VCFs anyway, the stronger version is to **merge first, then filter on per-site het fraction** (HDplot-style: count sites with het fraction above ~0.6, optionally combined with allele read-ratio).

## What your top loci actually are

The top 4 by density are all on `Binsu_Z`. The Z is 3.4% of loci but 28% of the top 18 — 8× enriched. Per-sample het counts at L1970 are sharply bimodal:

```
0-5 het sites:    56 samples
90-123 het sites: 120 samples
```

and per-site het fraction maxes at 0.70, never reaching 0.75. That's a Z/W gametolog pattern (W copies mapping onto the Z in one sex), not a plain collapsed paralog. So the filter is catching real problem loci — but the worst offenders are sex-linked, which is a separate decision: you may want to handle Z loci as a class rather than by an anonymous het threshold, and the bimodality gives you a free sex assignment for the 177 samples.

## Implementation issues

Ranked by how much they'd cost you:

1. **[scripts/04b_filter-excess-het.sh:186](scripts/04b_filter-excess-het.sh#L186)** — `rm -f "$vcf_out"/*.vcf.gz ...` with no guard that `$vcf_out` differs from `$vcf_in`. One typo in `run/1_main.md` deletes your filtered VCF set. Add an explicit check and exit.

2. **FASTA and VCF are filtered on different keys.** FASTA by a regex on the filename, VCFs by BED intervals from the het table. A locus present in one set but missing from the het table is silently *kept* in the FASTA and *dropped* from the VCFs. I verified the sets currently match exactly (1762 FASTA, 1762 het-table loci, 0 either way, no duplicate IDs), so this is latent — but it should fail loudly rather than diverge quietly.

3. **[04b:159](scripts/04b_filter-excess-het.sh#L159)** — `sed -E 's/.*_(L[0-9]+)_Binsu.*/\1/'`. If it doesn't match, sed returns the whole filename, which never matches the blacklist, so the locus is silently retained. All 1762 names parse today, but the failure mode is invisible. Check the result matches `^L[0-9]+$` and exit otherwise.

4. **[04b:140](scripts/04b_filter-excess-het.sh#L140)** — percentile is `int(NR*p/100)` with no interpolation, and the blacklist uses `$8 > cutoff`. Ties at the cutoff would silently change how many loci are dropped. Currently gives exactly 18, so no harm now.

5. **Denominator mismatch with the FASTA.** Het density is normalized by the full reference interval length from the BED, but ClipKIT trimmed many alignments to 5-201 bp. Loci are being judged on het in sequence that isn't in the final alignment. Also, the denominator isn't the *callable* length — loci with more Ns get artificially low density. Worth a deliberate decision rather than leaving implicit.

```
gap 0.57–0.66
```

## What I'd do

1. **Drop the 4 gametolog loci outright.** They're paralogs by any definition, and they're what made your het filter fire. This is the actual fix.

2. **Run `04b_filter-excess-het.sh` on autosomes only.** With Z excluded, the percentile cutoff is no longer distorted by sex linkage, and the remaining tail is real autosomal paralog signal. This also makes the percentile default much less objectionable.

3. **The 50 hemizygous loci depend on your downstream analysis:**
   - **Anything using genotypes or heterozygosity** (He, ROH, inbreeding, popgen): these must go, or females must be recalled as haploid. Right now female Z genotypes are called diploid-homozygous, which deflates per-sample heterozygosity in proportion to the Z fraction — that's the biggest silent bias in the current dataset, and it's confounded with sex.

Since Z is only 60/1762 loci (3.4%), the cheapest defensible route is to **drop all 60 from the main dataset** and analyze them separately if you want the sex-linked signal. You lose almost nothing.

4. **Keep the sex calls.** They're a free by-product and worth adding as a column to [metadata/metadata-final.tsv](metadata/metadata-final.tsv) — useful as a covariate, and for spotting the sex-ratio skew (~2:1 female).
